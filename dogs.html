Community dogs: fixed feeding spots

Dogs fed at one agreed spot, at one agreed time, by people they trust, stay in their own territory and can be caught for rabies vaccination and sterilisation. The law already asks the local body to set these spots up. This page lists the spots caregivers have registered, what the rules say, and a letter to ask your municipality to do its part.

Register the spot where you feed

1. Stand at the spotAway from school gates, children's play areas, building entrances, staircases and busy footpaths. Rule 20 asks for exactly this.
2. Say when and how manyOpen Register a feeding spot, give the name neighbours know you by, the time you feed and about how many dogs.
3. Keep it cleanWait while they eat, take away leftovers and plates. Rule 20(1)(iii) puts this on the feeder.
4. Help the ABC teamTick the box if you can help catch the dogs for rabies vaccination and sterilisation, as rule 20(1)(iv) allows.

A registered spot is the caregiver's proposal. It shows as "designated" only when a moderator has the local body's, RWA's or Animal Welfare Committee's order, letter or minutes.

What the rules say

Animal Birth Control Rules, 2023, rule 20: feeding of community animals

Notified 10 March 2023 (G.S.R. 193(E)) under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960. Gazette text (AWBI).

It shall be responsibility of the Resident Welfare Association or Apartment Owner Association or Local Body's representative of that area to make necessary arrangement for feeding of community animals residing in the premises or that area involving the person residing in that area or premises as the case may be, who feeds those animals …

They must ensure:

  1. feeding spots that are mutually agreed, keeping in mind the number of dogs and their territories, far from children's play areas, entry and exit points and staircases, where children and senior citizens are least likely to be;
  2. a feeding time when children, senior citizens and sports are least likely;
  3. that the designated feeder leaves no litter at the feeding spot;
  4. that designated feeders may volunteer for vaccination, catching and release of dogs for the Animal Birth Control programme.

If people disagree (rule 20(2)), an Animal Welfare Committee is formed: the Chief Veterinary Officer, the local police, the District SPCA or State Board, a recognised ABC organisation, the local body's veterinary officer, the complainant and the RWA or local body. Its decision on the feeding point is final; an appeal goes to the State Animal Welfare Board (rule 20(3)).

Also in the rules: each local authority has a Local Animal Birth Control Monitoring Committee (rule 9(4)); the local authority may set up an animal helpline and must record every complaint about dog bites or rabid dogs in a register (rule 16).

Supreme Court, order of 22 August 2025

In Re: "City Hounded by Strays, Kids Pay Price", Suo Motu W.P.(C) No. 5/2025. Order (sci.gov.in).

Municipal authorities shall forthwith commence an exercise for creating dedicated feeding spaces for the stray dogs in each municipal ward. … Gantries/notice boards shall be placed near such designated feeding areas, mentioning that stray dogs shall only be fed in such areas. Under no condition shall the feeding of stray dogs on the streets be permitted.

The same order asks each municipal authority to run a helpline for reporting violations (para 33(e)), says sterilised and vaccinated dogs go back to the area they were picked up from, except rabid or aggressive dogs (para 33(b)), and brings every State and Union Territory into the case (para 35).

Supreme Court, judgment of 19 May 2026

In the same case, as reported by SCC Online: at least one working ABC centre in every district; municipal bodies to remove stray dogs from the premises of schools, hospitals and railway stations to a designated shelter; High Courts to monitor compliance.
