Blood donation

Find blood, give blood, and the facts behind both. Every source is linked.

Find blood now

Live stock at blood banks is published by the government on eRaktKosh, the national blood availability portal. This page does not copy that stock data, so it is never out of date here. Open the official service page:

Check blood bank stock availability, eRaktKosh (services.india.gov.in)

For a real blood requirement, call the nearest hospital or blood bank directly. The eRaktKosh portal and its listing do not give a donor's contact details here.

Give blood

This site does not collect donor names or phone numbers for blood, and no donor is shown publicly here. To give blood, contact your nearest blood bank or hospital, or the official eRaktKosh portal above, which lists the blood banks.

Find blood: what to ask for

Give the blood bank the patient's blood group exactly as the hospital recorded it:

O+ · O- · A+ · A- · B+ · B- · AB+ · AB-

Check stock on the official eRaktKosh service above, then call the blood bank to confirm before you travel. If the group is not known, ask the hospital to test it. The list of official helplines is on Helplines and your rights.

Who can give blood

  • Age: 18 to 65 years.
  • Weight: 45 kg or more for whole blood. For apheresis, 50 kg or more for plasma; over 50 kg for platelets.
  • Wait between donations: whole blood every 90 days for men and every 120 days for women. Platelet apheresis every 2 weeks, up to 24 times a year.

Source: eRaktKosh, public index and eRaktKosh, donate blood (checked 15 October 2026). The eRaktKosh pages do not give a haemoglobin or blood pressure limit, so none is shown here. Ask the blood bank for the full check before donating.

How many blood centres are licensed

Licensed blood centres in India, by state and union territory, up to December 2021, from the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO). West Bengal has 153 (109 government, 44 other than government). All India has 3,782. Counts only; the list does not name each centre. (CDSCO, Blood Centre, released 5 January 2022.)

The facts

  • Every donation is tested. WHO recommends that all blood donations be screened for infections before use. (WHO, Blood safety and availability)
  • Voluntary donors are the safest. WHO says they are the safest group of donors, because blood-borne infections are least common among them. (WHO, Blood safety and availability)
