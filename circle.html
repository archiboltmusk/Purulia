The Circle

In Purulia, no problem stays in its own department. A girl married at sixteen becomes an anaemic mother; her child is born small into a home without a tap; the land gives one crop, so the father leaves to find work; and the family that stays behind is poorer and more likely to marry the next daughter young. This page follows that circle through the official figures, one step at a time.

Every number below comes from a government source, linked beside it. How each step feeds the next is our reading of those figures, not a finding of any one survey. Where nobody has published a number, we say so.

  10. When there is no work for half the year, people go where the work is

  11. Families who leave, and the ones left behind

    Nobody publishes how many people leave Purulia for work each year. The 2011 Census migration tables are fifteen years old, the 2021 Census was postponed and the next one has not reported, and neither the district nor the state publishes a count of seasonal migrants. What the figures above show is the pressure: half the workforce without year-round work, one crop, and a jobs guarantee that stopped for three years. A family whose earner is away, or in debt, is under more pressure to marry the next daughter early, and the circle starts again.

    The first demand is simple: count it. Every gram panchayat can record who leaves and when, as the jobs guarantee already records who works.

Who answers for each step

Health, water and agriculture are state subjects under the Constitution (Seventh Schedule, State List entries 6, 17 and 14), but most of the money for these schemes comes from the Centre, and the District Magistrate's office runs them on the ground. The MP chairs DISHA, the committee that reviews central schemes in the district every quarter, with the MLAs as members and the District Magistrate as convenor. So all three levels answer for every row.
