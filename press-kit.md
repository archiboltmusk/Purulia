## One-line description
A public map of civic problems across Purulia district — filed with a live photo and verified GPS, marked fixed only after independent on-site confirmation, with the full record open under CC BY 4.0.

## The problem
No public, verifiable, and accountable tool currently tracks civic problems across Purulia district end-to-end — from the initial report through to independent confirmation of a fix. For sourced, current figures on the district's specific development indicators, see the Ground Truth section of the main site (`data.html`) rather than this document — those numbers are kept current and cited there; repeating them here risks going stale or being misquoted out of context.

## What Parishkar Purulia does
- Citizens open the camera and take a photo — that's the whole flow. No account, no form, no category to pick.
- The server assigns everything else: the photo's location, ward or block, and category. GPS is checked server-side; the ward or block is computed from it, never from what the phone claims.
- Every report is public on a map, with photo and location, unless it's held for a human moderator (encroachment/illegal-activity reports, photos showing a face or flagged unsafe, or clearly off-topic photos).
- A cleanup claim requires independent on-site confirmations — 3 in Purulia town, 2 in villages — from people who weren't already involved and are on different mobile networks, before a report is marked resolved.
- A person whose reports a moderator repeatedly confirms as wrong is paused from filing new ones for 30 days — but never a person just flagged or disputed; only a moderator's own confirmed decision counts.
- All report data — text, location, category, status history — is open under CC BY 4.0, downloadable as CSV. Photos are not covered by that licence.

## Why it matters
- Most civic-complaint tools let an official close a complaint with a single click. This one doesn't: only independent, on-site, photographed confirmation resolves a report, and every moderator action is logged with a reason on the report's own public history.
- The verification rules run on the server, not the page — a modified or bypassed client can't skip them.
- The record persists independent of who holds office. An append-only public ledger of report fingerprints is published daily and mirrored to the Internet Archive, so the record can't be quietly edited later.
