Who runs the municipality, and where the money goes

A pothole or an overflowing drain in your ward is the end of a long chain: the state government, the elected board, the officers, the contractors, and the money that passes between them. This page lays out that chain for Purulia Municipality, with a source for every fact, and says plainly what has not been published.

Who controls whom

State government (Urban Development & Municipal Affairs Department)

Makes the rules for every municipality under the West Bengal Municipal Act, 1993, sends most of the money, and can dissolve an elected board under Section 431 of the Act if it finds "gross neglect" of the board's duties, after first giving the board a fair hearing.

↓
Board of Councillors

One elected councillor per ward. The councillors elect the Chairman, who heads the municipality with the Chairman-in-Council. The Chairman of Purulia Municipality is Nabendu Mahali.

↓
Officers and staff

The Executive Officer runs the office. Engineers (Assistant and Sub-Assistant Engineers) plan and check roads, drains and lights. The Sanitary Inspector and conservancy staff run garbage collection. Every report on Parishkar shows which of these officers answers for it.

↓
Contractors

Build and repair roads and drains after winning an e-tender (see below).

Administrator (only when the board is dissolved)

If the state dissolves the board, an officer it appoints runs the municipality instead of the elected councillors. In Purulia that was the Sub-Divisional Officer, Purulia Sadar.

What happened in 2025–26

Residents sent the state a mass complaint about sewer cleaning, drinking water, daily garbage disposal, potholed roads and street lights. On 19 November 2025 the state sent the board a show-cause notice. A District Magistrate's inquiry report followed on 9 December 2025, and on 16 December 2025 the Governor dissolved the Board of Councillors under Section 431(2) and appointed the SDO, Purulia Sadar, as Administrator. The Administrator's term was later extended.

Councillors challenged it. On 29 June 2026 the Calcutta High Court (Justice Raja Basu Chowdhury, Rabishankar Das v. State of West Bengal, WPA 11873 of 2026) set aside the dissolution, the Administrator's appointment and its extension. The court held the board had never been given the inquiry report the state relied on, and that the show-cause notice came before the state had formed any opinion of "gross neglect", as the Act requires. The elected board is back in charge.

The court ruled on the procedure, not on whether services were adequate. The complaints that started it are still the complaints people report on this site every day.

Source: LiveLaw, 29 June 2026; Sangbad Pratidin (dissolution). We have not seen a report of an appeal; tell us if there is one.

Where the money comes from

  • State government grants. The largest share. Some is untied (the municipality chooses how to spend it, following the State Finance Commission's formula); much more comes as salary grants and as money for a named project, such as street lights under the state's Green City Mission.
  • Central schemes. Money for a stated purpose, for example Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 for waste, and Finance Commission grants, part of which must go to sanitation and water.
  • Its own income. Mainly property tax on land and buildings, plus trade licence fees, market and advertisement fees, and any user charge for services.

Money given for a named project has to be spent on that project, and the municipality must send the funding department a utilisation certificate saying how it was spent. That is why ward-wise records exist, even though Purulia Municipality does not publish them.

How much Purulia received from each source, and how much it spent in each ward, is not published. The RTI below asks for it. City-level figures for many West Bengal towns are on the Centre's CityFinance portal.

How a road or drain job gets done

  1. Approval and money. The funding department approves the work and sanctions a budget for it.
  2. E-tender. A Notice Inviting e-Tender (NIeT) goes up on the state's portal, wbtenders.gov.in, with the estimated value, earnest money and the days allowed to finish. Anyone can read it there.
  3. Bids and work order. Bids are opened on a set date, normally the lowest valid bid wins, and the municipality issues a work order with the completion deadline. If no valid bid comes, the tender is issued again as a "2nd call".
  4. Work, measurement and payment. The engineer measures the finished work, the contractor is paid against it, and the municipality sends a utilisation certificate for the money to the department that gave it.

A real example from Ward 1: reconstruction of the cement concrete road from the Tanishq showroom via Sital Bauri's house and Yuva Club, Goda Nala, D.B. Road bye-lane. Tender value ₹12,52,084, 30 days to complete. It was a 2nd call; bids closed on 8 March 2024 and were opened on 11 March 2024.

Source: NIeT No. WBMAD/PURULIA/NIeT-187/2023-24, tender ID 2024_MAD_672406_2 on wbtenders.gov.in (copy). Who won, at what price, and whether the road was finished on time are not published; ask for them with question 3 below.

Spotted a job in your ward? Report the road or drain with a photo on Parishkar. A report of a road that was just re-laid and is already broken is exactly what a tender record can be checked against.

Why it has to be asked ward by ward

Purulia Municipality's own records put its waste fleet at 840 sanitation workers (permanent and contractual), 12 tractors, 5 pick-up vans and 3 compactors. Yet a 2022 GIS study of the town found collection points "neither equal nor proportionate with respect to the population of all the wards", said collection and disposal "lacks planning", and found not a single dustbin in ward 21.

In July 2026 the National Green Tribunal named Purulia among 14 West Bengal municipalities that cannot transport all their waste; together those 14 leave 177.03 tonnes a day uncollected. The order does not say how much of that is Purulia's. Town-wide totals hide which wards get the workers, the bins and the money, and which don't. Only ward-wise figures show that.

Sources: Palit & Patra, International Journal of Health Sciences 6(S3), 2022, citing Purulia Municipality (2019); NGT order of 22 July 2026, O.A. 606/2018.

Ask for ward-wise spending (RTI)

Any Indian citizen can send this. Fill in your name and address, attach the ₹10 fee (postal order or court-fee stamp), and post it or hand it in at the municipality office. The officer must reply within 30 days. When the reply comes, send it to us and we will publish the figures here with the reply as the source.

To, The Public Information Officer, Purulia Municipality, Purulia, West Bengal Subject: Request for information under Section 6(1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005 Please provide the following information for the financial years 2023-24, 2024-25 and 2025-26: 1. The amount budgeted and the amount actually spent on solid waste management, ward by ward, split into salaries and wages, vehicles and fuel, dustbins and equipment, contracts, and processing or disposal. 2. The funds received for solid waste management from each source (State Finance Commission and other state grants, Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0, Finance Commission grants, any other scheme), the amount spent from each, and copies of the utilisation certificates sent for them. 3. A list of every work order issued for roads, drains, street lights and solid waste management, giving for each: ward, name of work, tender ID, sanctioned amount, name of the contractor, accepted amount, date of work order, completion deadline, actual completion date, and amount paid so far. 4. The number of sanitation workers (permanent and contractual) and collection vehicles deployed in each ward as on the date of this application. 5. Whether the municipality charges households or businesses any fee for garbage collection. If so, a copy of the resolution or notification fixing it, the rates, the amount collected in each year, and what it was spent on. 6. Copies of the annual budget and the annual accounts (audited, where audit is complete) of Purulia Municipality for these years.
