// 06 — The First Five

Five People. Five Conversations.

Not a broadcast. Not a mass email. Five targeted messages this week. Each one moves one real thing. This is how blueprints become projects.

1
The District Collector
Or the MLA / MP with real local access
The person who can make existing systems move: land mutations, Forest Rights Act titles, Silpasathi applications, restarted rural works. Stable tenure matters — continuity is what compounds.
"Read the politicians section. Tell me what would need to be true for this to happen in your term."
2
A Solar Developer
A project head at a national renewable energy firm
Signed solar developers create visible activity — construction and lease income for landowners who agree to it. That visible proof changes the district's narrative faster than anything else.
"Purulia has dry laterite land and a state single window for clearances. Would your team take a site visit?"
3
The Purulia-Origin Senior
A director, VP, or partner — anywhere in India or abroad
One person with procurement power who is emotionally connected to Purulia can open supply chain conversations, make introductions, and invest — often all three, once they see the plan.
"Read the diaspora section. I want to know if you'd come to the first Pravasi Sammelan."
4
The Journalist
A state correspondent, development reporter, or regional editor
A single well-researched deep dive on local policy execution reaches the exact people who will never see this website. Media scrutiny creates administrative accountability.
"According to NFHS-5, Purulia has one of West Bengal's highest child anaemia rates and lowest female literacy rates. The story is the gap between the schemes that exist and what reaches the villages."
5
The Doctor or Specialist
Ideally Purulia-origin, practicing in any Indian city
One specialist who publicly commits to setting up in Purulia signals to the next specialist that it is a real career option. Momentum is made of arrivals as much as announcements.
"Read the doctors section. Purulia now has two medical colleges and no specialist ecosystem around them yet. Would you consider building one?"
// 07 — The First Move

18 months.
One desk in the Collectorate.

These five actions could unlock the rest. Sequenced deliberately. Each creates conditions for the next. Most need little new money — mainly will and follow-through.

"The entire system depends on getting the first anchor investors. Everything before is preparation. Everything after is momentum."

1
Clear pending land-record and Forest Rights Act cases
Land records are online on Banglarbhumi. Finish whatever is still pending — record updates, mutations, land-use conversions, forest-rights titles — through a fast-track cell at the Collectorate, and publish the pending list so progress is visible. FRA implementation in Purulia has been documented as ad-hoc, with Gram Sabha approvals bypassed and claims rejected on documentation pretexts.
Start Now
2
Publish a Purulia tracker on the Silpasathi single window
The state portal already carries over a hundred licences and approvals. A district desk helps applicants use it and publishes every Purulia application with its days pending. Low cost.
Month 1–6
3
Bring in the first solar developers
Start with rooftop and institutional systems, then larger projects on land whose owners agree — with Forest Rights Act checks first.
Month 3–12
4
Expand the medical colleges already here
Purulia has two medical colleges. Push NMC for PG seat approvals, recruit specialist faculty, and build the private practice ecosystem around them.
Month 1–3
5
Repair the five best government schools
Power, toilets, library, teacher monitoring. Roughly ₹15–25L per school (our estimate). Signals to families that staying is viable.
Month 1–6
2040
"Every district that breaks out of systemic poverty has a tipping point. It isn't magic; it is the moment when infrastructure aligns with local enterprise and people decide to stay...

Purulia's turning point story is not written yet.
That is not a problem.
That is the opportunity."

The solar farms on laterite land. The Dokra artist getting her first export order. The engineer who came back to start an O&M company. The architect designing a railway station that makes people feel something when they arrive.

Document all of it. In 2040, this becomes the Purulia Story. That story starts with what happens in the next eighteen months.
