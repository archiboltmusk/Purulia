Public works: who must fix it, free

When a government road, drain or building is finished, the contractor stays responsible for its defects for a fixed time, the defect liability period. If it cracks, sinks or leaks in that time, the contractor must repair it at their own cost. The board at the site often says who did the work and how long that period is. This page keeps those boards, shows which works are still covered, and gives you an RTI request for the work's papers.

Record a works board

1. Stand at the boardThe citizen information board at the site of a road, drain, culvert or building. Open Record a works board with location on.
2. Photograph itOne clear photo, taken live, where every line can be read.
3. Copy what it saysThe name of the work, the office that did it, and if written, the contractor, work order number, cost, completion date and defect liability period. Copy only what is on the board.
4. Report the defectWhen a report on the map is close to a work still under warranty, its page says so, with a link here.

The contractor's name is shown only as it is written on the board. A defect is not proof of wrongdoing by anyone; it is a repair the contract already pays for.

Every recorded work

Works still under warranty first. "Warranty until" is the completion date on the board plus the period on the board; it is shown only when the board gives both.

What the defect liability period means

For a set time after a work is completed, any defect that shows up is the contractor's to fix, at their own expense. If they do not, the engineer in charge can get it repaired by others and take the cost out of the contractor's dues or security deposit. The length of the period depends on the kind of work and is written in the work's contract.

West Bengal Public Works Department: clause 17 of the tender form

Notification No. 5784-PW/L&A/2M-175/2017, dated 12 September 2017, Law & Arbitration Cell, Public Works Department, Government of West Bengal, substituting clause 17 of the Conditions of Contract in West Bengal Form No. 2911/2911(i)/2911(ii). Text of the notification (WBXPress).

… if any damage shall happen to the work from any cause whatsoever or any imperfection become apparent in it at any time whether during its execution or within a period of three months or one year or three years or five years, as the case may be (depending upon the nature of the work as described in the explanation appended hereto) hereinafter referred to as the Defect Liability Period, from the actual date of completion of work as per completion certificate issued by the Engineer-in-Charge, the contractor shall make the same good at his own expense, or in default, the Engineer-in-Charge may cause the same to be made good by other workmen and deduct the expense … from any sums … that may be then, or at any time thereafter become due to the contractor from the Government or from his security deposit …

The same clause says the security deposit is returned to the contractor in parts as the period runs out. Later orders may have changed the periods for some kinds of work, and municipalities and panchayats use their own contracts, so the period that counts is the one in the work order, which is often also written on the board. That is the period shown on this page.

Ask for the work's papers under RTI

Pick a work to fill in the request, add your name and address, and copy it. Send it yourself to the Public Information Officer of the office that did the work, with the fee your state's RTI rules ask for, and keep the receipt. Parishkar does not send it for you.
