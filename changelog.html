What's new

Every change to Parishkar, newest first · Rules changes are also listed on Rules

26 September 2026

  • Put a school on the map, or fix its pin. Standing at a school? Your school check puts it on the map where you are. If a pin looks wrong, tap Wrong place? Fix it on the Schools page and then 📍 The school is here. The pin follows where most recent visitors stood, so one wrong tap can't move it far.
  • School map. The Schools page now has a map: each block is shaded by how many of its schools residents have checked, and schools with a known location show as pins, green, amber or red by their score, or grey if nobody has checked them yet. Tap a block to list its schools.
  • Schools near you. When you tap 🏫 Check a school, your block is filled in from your location and the schools around you are listed first, like nearby stations in a rail app. Each check also teaches the map where that school is, so the next person finds it faster.
  • Check a school. Standing at a school? Open the menu and tap 🏫 Check a school: pick it from the official list of all 4,040 schools, answer six questions (water, toilets, boundary wall, electricity, mid-day meal kitchen, building) and take a photo. The new Schools page shows every checked school worst first, the ones nobody has checked yet, a ready-to-send RTI to the Samagra Shiksha office for any school with problems, and a link to offer help on Vidyanjali.
  • A fix has to last. If a problem is reported again at the same spot within 14 days of being marked fixed, the old report says Didn't last and links to the new one, and the fix no longer counts in analytics, the leaderboard or the weekly digest.
  • Every school in the district is now on record. All 4,040 government-listed schools in Purulia (by block, panchayat and village) are loaded, so school checks can be matched to a real school.
  • Forests from space. The analytics page now links to Global Forest Watch's satellite map of Purulia, where you can see where tree cover is disappearing. If you can see illegal felling or mining on the spot, report it with a photo.
  • Voice typing. When you report a problem, tap 🎤 Say what's wrong and speak in Bengali, Hindi or English. Your phone turns it into text for the description. It uses your phone's own voice typing.
  • Old photos take less space. Ninety days after a problem is fixed, its photos are saved at a smaller size so the free storage lasts for years. The before and after photos stay on the record.
  • Written addresses. Reports now show the nearest named road or place from OpenStreetMap, like "NC Dasgupta Road · near Purulia Haat Tala Baazar", on the map, in lists and in the complaint text you send the municipality. Your own landmark still comes first.
  • Road reports are checked. If a road report's photo shows no road at all, a moderator looks at it before it stays on the map.
  • Faster cleanup confirmation. If the photo check sees no rubbish in a garbage or drain cleanup photo, one person at the spot can confirm it instead of the full number. If nobody confirms or disputes it within 3 days, it closes on the photo check alone, and the map and analytics say so. Anyone at the spot can still dispute it.
  • Fixed: the Continue button on the admin sign-in was missing on some iPhones. Pressing Go on the keyboard now signs in too.
  • Moderators: the admin page now shows counts at the top (waiting, hidden, kept, flags) and tabs for Waiting, Hidden and Kept after review, so a mistaken hide can be undone in one tap. Buttons are bigger on phones.
  • Up to 3 photos per report. After the first photo, tap Add another photo to show the problem from more angles. Every photo is checked the same way as the first.
  • Weekly ward digest by email. Every Monday morning, last week's numbers for every ward — new, fixed, unresolved, overdue, and what has waited longest — go out by email, starting with the Parishkar team.
  • "Still there?" If you've allowed location and you're within 100 m of a problem reported at least 2 days ago, the map asks whether it's still there. One tap counts as an on-site sighting, or starts a cleanup confirmation if it's gone.
  • Moderators get an email when a report is flagged, so flags don't wait for someone to open the admin page.
  • Faster first load. On a slow phone connection, reports now show in about 3 seconds instead of 5: the page no longer waits for the map to download before showing everything else.
  • Fixed: on phones, the Purulia 2040 pages scrolled sideways and the WhatsApp button sat at the bottom of the page instead of floating in the corner.
  • Flash and zoom on the camera. Tap ⚡ to light up a dark spot (Android phones with Chrome). Tap 1× / 2× to zoom in; phones with a zoom lens also get 4×.
  • Moderation is now public. The analytics page shows, month by month, how many reports were hidden or kept, how many flags came in, how many fake cleanups were thrown out, and how many people are on the moderation team.
  • Copy complaint text. Under "Take it further" on any report, one tap copies the full complaint — place, days open, map and report link — ready to paste into CPGRAMS or any government portal.
  • Run Parishkar in your own town. A step-by-step setup guide for civic groups and municipalities elsewhere. The code is free to copy.
  • MLA and MP leaderboard. The analytics page now groups every report by assembly seat and Lok Sabha seat, across all nine seats of Purulia district, with the MLA (2026 election) and MP (2024) named.
  • Share as a picture. The share button on a report now sends a picture card to WhatsApp — the photo, how many days it's been open, who is responsible and the MLA — with the link. On a computer, the picture is saved and the link copied.
  • Before and after on the map. Tap ✓ fixed on the map to swipe through recent fixes — the original photo next to the neighbour's cleanup photo, and how fast it was fixed.
  • Weekly ward digest. Every ward and block gets a weekly page: new reports, verified fixes, what's overdue and what has waited longest — ready to share every Monday. Open it from a ward on the map with This week.
  • This page. Every change is listed here from now on.

25 September 2026

  • The map is the whole screen. Swiping and zooming no longer fight the page. Everything else is under ☰ More, and filters are behind one Filters button.
  • The map no longer jumps to your location if you've already started moving it.
  • Nearby reports filter to see what's been reported around you.
  • Every flag reaches a moderator. Before, a report needed three flags from established accounts before anyone looked at it.
  • Admins and moderators. Moderators review and hide; only admins can delete a report permanently, see sign-ups, or change the team.
  • Watch a report to get a notification when it changes, and see all your own reports under Your reports.
  • "Resolved by 3 confirmers" — every fixed report now says how it was confirmed.
  • RTI application generator for reports that are past their deadline.
  • Report from anywhere in Purulia district, not just the town. Village reports are grouped by CD block.
  • Photos only from the live camera for reports, cleanups, confirmations and disputes — no gallery photos.
  • Quick report: the camera opens straight away; take the photo and send.
  • Location fixes: the report is placed where the photo was taken, even if you opened the app somewhere else.
  • Categories fixed: reports were sometimes filed as "Other" by mistake. Photos are now checked and the category corrected automatically, and you can flag a wrong category.
  • Reporting pauses for accounts whose reports moderators keep hiding.
  • Kasa is now Parishkar Purulia. Rules page published.
  • Public analytics, a How we count page, list search and volunteer groups.
