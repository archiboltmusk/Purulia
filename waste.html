Where Purulia's waste goes

Garbage on the street is the last link of a chain. Every town's waste needs five links, from your home to a safe landfill. When one is missing, the pile on the corner comes back however often it is swept. This page lists each link, what the rules require, and what Purulia Municipality has published about it.

1

Sorting at home

Wet waste (food, peels) and dry waste (plastic, paper, glass) go in separate bins. The national Solid Waste Management Rules make this the duty of every household and shop. Without it, nothing further down the chain can be composted or recycled.

What you can do today: keep two bins, and hand them over separately.

Purulia: no published figure for how many homes sort
2

Door-to-door collection

A worker or vehicle takes both bins from every house, every day, without mixing them. The rules make the municipality responsible for this. If collection is irregular, people stop sorting and start dumping at the corner.

What the app shows: garbage reports by ward on the Analytics page, and spots that keep filling up.

Purulia: no published route or schedule by ward
3

Transfer and sorting

A covered place where dry waste is sorted into what can be sold or recycled (a Material Recovery Facility), before anything goes further.

Purulia: not published
4

Processing

Wet waste becomes compost or biogas. Dry waste is recycled. In April 2025 West Bengal's Urban Development Department said processing had started in 14 of the state's 128 municipalities, with a target of 91 by March 2026.

Source: APAC News Network, 28 April 2025. Purulia is not named in that list.

Purulia: no published plant, capacity or start date
5

Safe landfill

Only what cannot be composted or recycled should go to a lined, covered sanitary landfill. Open dumps leak into groundwater and catch fire. The same April 2025 report says 42 dumping grounds in 28 West Bengal municipalities had been cleared by bio-mining.

Purulia: dumpsite size and clean-up plan not published

Ask for the missing answers (RTI)

Anyone who is an Indian citizen can send this. Fill in your name and address, attach the ₹10 fee (postal order or court-fee stamp), and post it or hand it in at the municipality office. The officer must reply within 30 days. When the reply comes, send it to us and we will add the facts to this page with the reply as the source.

What every one of us can do

The chain above is the municipality's job, but the first link is ours. Keep two bins. Hand waste to the collector instead of leaving it at the corner. Adopt the spot outside your shop, school or club, and report a pile with a photo so the pattern is on the record.
