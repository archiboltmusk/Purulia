The rules

Rules as of 25 September 2026 · Written for anyone — reporter, moderator, or official — to read and know exactly what's allowed. The mechanics behind these rules are on the Methodology page; this page is the conduct summary.

You are being trusted with a public record. What you file becomes part of a permanent, public document about Purulia district. Every rule here exists to keep that record accurate and free from manipulation.

For everyone

  1. One report per problem. If a problem's already been reported, don't file it again — the server links a same-category report within 25 m of an open one to the original automatically, and counts you as a witness rather than a duplicate.
  2. You must be there. Every report's photo is taken live, at the location, with the camera on this page — never from your gallery, a friend, or the internet. If a photo can't be confirmed live, it isn't rejected outright, but it waits for a moderator before it's published rather than appearing immediately.
  3. Your location is your location. The GPS determines the ward or block; you don't choose it. If GPS isn't available, you can pin the spot on the map by hand as a fallback — that pin is recorded as manually placed, not as a GPS fix, and is treated accordingly.
  4. No photos with people's faces or number plates. The platform is public. If Vision detects a face in a report photo, it's held for a moderator rather than published automatically. If the problem is with a person, not a place, this isn't the right tool for it.
  5. No defamation. If you add any details, don't accuse a named person of a crime. "Garbage not collected for 10 days" is fine; "the councillor is a thief" is not. Reports are citizen allegations, not proven facts — stick to what you saw.
  6. No abuse, hate, or harassment. No slurs, threats, or religious or caste-based language. The platform is for civic problems, not for attacking people.
  7. One vote per person per report. "I saw this too" and on-site ratings count once per account.
  8. No fake cleanups. Don't claim a cleanup you didn't do or see, or submit a photo of a different location or an old photo. The verification rules are built to catch this, and false claims are logged permanently.
  9. Flags are for real problems, not for silencing reports. Flagging doesn't need a photo, but it does need a reason, and flags from brand-new accounts don't weigh — it takes several established accounts, on different networks, for a flag to mark a report "under review." Flagging can never hide a report by itself; only a moderator can do that.
  10. The record is permanent. Every report, resolution, flag, and moderator decision is logged permanently. You can't delete your own report or edit it after submission.

For reporters

  1. You're responsible for what you file. Submitting a report confirms it's true to the best of your knowledge, that the photo was taken live at the location, and that you're not filing it to harass anyone.
  2. You're anonymous by default. The platform doesn't publish your name, your device, or your exact identity. A published report shows the photo, its location (ward or block), its category, and the timestamp — nothing that identifies who filed it.
  3. You can flag your own report as wrongly categorised. If your report should have been filed under a different category, flag it with a suggestion — a moderator reviews and makes the actual change. You can't change the location once filed.

For moderators

  1. Don't act on your own ward. If a report is from the ward or block you live in, leave it for another moderator.
  2. Every decision is logged with a reason. Hiding a report, correcting a category, or restoring one — the reason is recorded and visible to other moderators.
  3. You can't edit a report. Moderators can hide, restore, or correct the category. Nothing on this platform — not even a moderator — can change a report's description, photo, or location; there is no edit capability in the system at all, by design.
  4. You don't get reporter identities. The tools you use don't surface who filed a report. If you somehow learn it anyway, you don't share it with anyone — the platform's safety depends on this.
  5. No money, gifts, or favours. If anyone offers you money, gifts, or favours connected to a moderation decision, report it immediately.
  6. Respond within a reasonable time. If you can't get to the queue, hand it off to the other moderator — reports shouldn't sit because one person is busy.

For officials and authorities

  1. You have a right of reply. If a report names your ward, department, or work, you can submit a response, published unedited alongside the report. E-mail the Grievance Officer to submit one.
  2. You can't close a report by saying so. No official, party worker, or moderator can mark a report resolved by declaring it fixed. Only independent on-site confirmation — 3 confirmations in Purulia town, 2 in villages — resolves a report.
  3. A report can be escalated. Once a report has been open past its target resolution time, anyone viewing it — not only the original reporter, who stays anonymous and can't be identified or contacted — can generate a pre-filled RTI application asking the responsible office what's been done. It's a draft for a human to review, sign, and file themselves; the platform doesn't file it automatically or on a timer, and doesn't (yet) generate the further legal-appeal steps beyond that first RTI.

For the platform itself

  1. The data is open. Every report, resolution, and flag's history is published under CC BY 4.0. Anyone can download, cite, or republish it.
  2. The rules are public. This page is on the website. Any change is published here with a date; material changes are noted at the top of the page.
  3. The platform is non-political. Not affiliated with, and does not endorse or oppose, any party or candidate. It publishes facts about civic problems and their status — nothing more.
  4. The platform is not the government. It can't fix problems. It can make them visible and help escalate them through the legal channels that exist for that — RTI, the grievance portals, and eventually the officials themselves. Set your expectations accordingly.

What happens when a rule is broken

SituationWhat actually happens
A moderator hides a report as false or abusiveHidden the 1st and 2nd time, no further consequence. A 3rd report hidden by a moderator within 30 days pauses new reporting from that account for 30 days. Confirming, disputing, and flagging other people's reports stays open throughout.
A cleanup claim is rejected as fakeThe claim is rejected and logged. 3 rejected claims within 30 days pauses claiming (not reporting) from that account for 30 days — a moderator can also block claiming outright for a pattern of abuse.
An account files a lot of low-value flagsFlags from new or low-activity accounts already don't count toward moving a report to "under review" — there's no separate proof requirement to add on top, since a flag can't hide anything by itself.
Abuse or a named accusation gets throughThe report is hidden by a moderator with a reason on the record. It is never edited — nothing on this platform can edit a report's content once submitted.
A moderator acts on their own ward, or leaks an identityHandled by the Society directly — removal from the decision, formal review, and for a deliberate identity leak, removal as a moderator and, where warranted, legal action. This isn't something the software enforces; it's enforced by whoever's running the platform.

This table describes the mechanisms as built. If you think one of these has been applied wrongly to your account, write to the Grievance Officer.

The principle behind all the rules

What you file becomes part of a permanent, public document about Purulia — one that will outlive this platform, this phone, and probably all of us. It's the only record of its kind for this district. Every rule exists to protect that record: to keep it accurate, permanent, and free from manipulation. Break the rules, and you break the record. Follow them, and you help build something that matters for years, not just for one complaint.
