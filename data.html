// The Honest Numbers — Official Data First

The Numbers Are Hard. That Is Exactly Why They Must Be Right.

Official figures come first (NFHS-5, government records, court orders), each with its source. Figures from single academic studies are labelled "one study". Anything we could not confirm has been removed. Checked September 2026.

Child Anaemia
77.9%
Children aged 6–59 months who are anaemic — up from 66.8% in NFHS-4 (2015–16). The highest of the 20 West Bengal districts in NFHS-5; the median district is 69.3%. 76.7% of women aged 15–49 are anaemic too.
Source: NFHS-5 District Fact Sheet, Puruliya (2019–21), IIPS / MoHFW
Underweight Children
46.3%
Children under 5 who are underweight — the highest of the 20 West Bengal districts (median 32.1%), though down from 58.2% in NFHS-4. 36.9% are stunted and 29.4% wasted (7.9% severely wasted).
Source: NFHS-5 District Fact Sheet, Puruliya (2019–21), IIPS / MoHFW
Women's Literacy
61.0%
Women aged 15–49 who are literate — the lowest of the 20 West Bengal districts (median 74.0%). Only 26.9% of women have 10 or more years of schooling. 63.6% of girls and women aged 6+ have ever attended school, up from 53.6%.
Source: NFHS-5 District Fact Sheet, Puruliya (2019–21), IIPS / MoHFW
Improved Sanitation
29.2%
People living in households that use an improved toilet — up from 12.1% in NFHS-4, real progress, but still the lowest of the 20 West Bengal districts (median 70.0%). The district was declared open-defecation free; NFHS measures something different (the type of toilet a household uses), so both can be true.
Source: NFHS-5 District Fact Sheet, Puruliya (2019–21), IIPS / MoHFW
Clean Cooking Fuel
23.2%
Households cooking with clean fuel such as LPG, up from 6.8%. Three in four homes still cook on wood, dung or coal, which falls hardest on women and small children.
Source: NFHS-5 District Fact Sheet, Puruliya (2019–21), IIPS / MoHFW
Child Marriage
37.0%
Women aged 20–24 who were married before 18, down from 43.7% in NFHS-4. Institutional births have risen to 89.8% (from 72.9%).
Source: NFHS-5 District Fact Sheet, Puruliya (2019–21), IIPS / MoHFW
Infant Mortality (reported)
38 per 1,000
Infant deaths per 1,000 live births as reported by the district (38.34; maternal mortality 176). The district gives no year and counts reported deaths, a different method from the national SRS survey, so treat it as indicative. For comparison, SRS puts West Bengal at 19 (2020) and India at 27 (2021).
Source: Health page, purulia.gov.in; SRS Statistical Report (Registrar General of India)
Rural Jobs Guarantee
Paused 2022–25
The Centre stopped MGNREGA funds to West Bengal on 9 March 2022 under Section 27 of the Act, with ₹3,082.52 crore in pending liabilities at that date (wages ₹1,457 cr, materials ₹1,608 cr). The Supreme Court upheld the Calcutta High Court order to resume it (27 Oct 2025); work restarted from 6 Dec 2025 with conditions. From 1 July 2026 MGNREGA is replaced nationally by the VB–G RAM G Act (125 days guaranteed).
Source: Govt. reply in Rajya Sabha, 1 Aug 2025; Supreme Court, 27 Oct 2025; MoRD order, 6 Dec 2025; VB–G RAM G Act, 2025
Aspirational District?
No
Purulia is not in NITI Aayog's Aspirational Districts Programme. The listed West Bengal districts are Dakshin Dinajpur, Maldah, Murshidabad and Nadia. Earlier versions of this page said otherwise; that was wrong.
Source: List of Aspirational Districts, MSME / NITI Aayog
Rare Earth Minerals
Exploration stage
The Geological Survey of India has explored parts of Purulia for rare earth elements at the advanced G2 stage under the National Critical Minerals Mission. We could not find a completed auction of any Purulia rare-earth block. Any future mining must come after Forest Rights Act claims and gram sabha consent, not before.
Source: Deccan Herald, 2025 (GSI G2 exploration in Purulia); National Critical Minerals Mission, Jan 2025
Single-Window Clearances
Silpasathi
West Bengal already has an online single window for industry, Silpasathi (WBIDC), with 145 licences, approvals and renewals. The job in Purulia is to make it work locally, not to build a parallel office.
Source: silpasathi.wb.gov.in
Forest Income (one study)
47% of income
In a 2020–21 survey of 70 tribal households in forest-fringe villages, firewood, sal leaves and sabai grass made up 47% of household income. It is a small sample, not a district figure, but it shows why Forest Rights Act implementation matters.
Source: Agroforestry Systems, Springer 2025 (70-household field study)
Drought Risk (one study)
Drought-prone
Purulia sits on hard rock on the Chotanagpur plateau, where groundwater is scarce and most rain comes in the monsoon. One 2023 modelling study classes about two-thirds of the district as severe-to-extreme drought risk. The exact share depends on method; the need for water storage does not.
Source: Environmental Monitoring & Assessment, Springer 2023; CGWB aquifer mapping, Purulia
Wasteland (one study)
28.41% of WB total
A 2021 study mapped wastelands — rocky, gravelly, mining, badland and degraded forest — covering 7.51% of the district and 28.41% of West Bengal's total. The maps exist; they have not been used for district-scale restoration.
Source: Mapping and Reclamation of Wastelands in Drought-Prone Purulia, Springer 2021
Removed until sourced
Earlier versions of this page quoted figures we could not confirm at an official source: per-capita income (its year was wrong), BPL share, child labour and dropout rates, women without bank accounts, power transmission loss, irrigation coverage, solar irradiance, industrial land prices, a "62.4% child malnutrition" figure, an HDI of 0.31, a 71% tribal dropout rate, a migration survey and quick-commerce availability. They are gone until a primary source is found. Tell us if you have one.

⚠ The Five Things Most Likely to Make This Fail — And the Specific Answers
Unvarnished Risks
  • PESA tribal land constraints: Schedule V area restrictions mean no non-tribal can buy land without complex Gram Sabha approval. Every industrial zone must navigate this — and most don't know how.
  • Political transfer cycles: The average District Collector tenure in WB is under 18 months. Every transformative programme needs 5–7 years of continuity. The system is designed to prevent exactly that.
  • Contractor capture: Infrastructure funds in WB routinely disappear into substandard work with zero accountability. Roads are built on paper. Substations upgraded in reports. Physical reality diverges from official records.
  • WBSEDCL monopoly inertia: Industrial power connections take 8+ months. Every manufacturer who runs on diesel generators adds ₹8–12/unit to their costs. This single fact has killed more investments than any policy failure.
  • Brain drain compounds itself: 50.7% labour force participation rate (2023–24) disguises structural unemployment. The educated leave. The uneducated have no leverage. The cycle does not break without deliberate intervention.
The Specific Countermeasures
  • PESA answer: 30-year land leasing — tribal landowners retain title, receive annual income, no acquisition law triggered. Gujarat's solar projects operate entirely on this model. It is legally clean and community-positive.
  • Transfer answer: Written 3-year tenure guarantee signed by MP, MLA, and DC. Joint vision document — politically harder to abandon mid-term when all three signatories are on record.
  • Contractor answer: Third-party quality audits, GPS-tagged photo documentation at every construction stage, public contractor blacklisting with actual enforcement. Already piloted in PMGSY — needs district-wide expansion.
  • Power answer: Captive solar microgrids for industrial zones — complete independence from WBSEDCL grid. Companies generate their own power. State grid becomes backup, not dependency. This is implementable in 18 months.
  • Brain drain answer: The Young Leaders Fellowship deliberately creates a peer group before the ecosystem exists. 20 talented people placed together generates social gravity. Peer effects are as powerful as economic incentives.
